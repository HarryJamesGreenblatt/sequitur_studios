## What happened

The Producer, thumbing through **_Directing the Story: Professional Storytelling and
Storyboarding Techniques for Live Action and Animation_** (Francis Glebas), noticed its
**storytelling** half reads as *directing story elements* rather than board mechanics —
and proposed pulling it in to supplement the driving **Director** agent and the
**Screenwriter**, paired with a favourable **production / art-design** source in the same
abridgement run. This is the right instinct at the right time: both feed the exact two
plan-phase producers that are the last blockers on the `0036` first slice
(**plan → {treatment + poster} → gate**). This entry records the decision; the import,
gate, and mapping happen when the deliverables arrive (the `0015` staging pattern), and
the per-chapter `reference/` abridgement is **deferred to its own designated session(s)**.

## Decisions

1. **_Directing the Story_ (Glebas) — take the *storytelling* half, drop the
   *storyboarding* half.** We already have a storyboarding source (Paez & Jew's
   *Professional Storyboarding*, `0018`), so Glebas' board-mechanics chapters are
   redundant. His distinctive contribution is the **story → image bridge**: visual
   dramatic storytelling, clarity of staging, the story spine, character objectives,
   sequence/beat construction — a niche neither Rabiger (broad directing) nor Williams
   (classification) nor Paez & Jew (board drawing) fills. This is a **filtered
   extraction** (the same call as dropping Prof. Storyboarding ch. 11–12).

2. **Glebas grounds *existing* seats, not a new one.** Like Rabiger's *Directing*, it is
   a cross-cutting reference that attaches to the **Director** (interpreting a story beat
   into a readable image) and the **Screenwriter** (the dramatic spine the `0036`
   *treatment* output needs) — its own `artifacts/` folder, but **no new `crew/`
   module**. It strengthens the Director `PersonaJudgment` (B tier) and the Screenwriter
   treatment; it does not add a department.

3. **The production-design source finally seats the Production Designer — the 8th gap.**
   `0036` left open "which text grounds key art / the poster." Recommendation:
   **Michael Rizzo, _The Art Direction Handbook for Film & Television_** — *systematic /
   reference* (art-department process, roles, deliverables: concept art, set design,
   colour scripts), which clears our standing "prefer textbook over memoir" bar
   (the Yewdall lesson). Final pick is the Producer's; whatever lands, it grounds a real
   **Production Designer** seat over `ImageStudio`.

4. **Production design (the world's look) ≠ key art (the marketing one-sheet).** These
   are different crafts. Our Production Designer seat (Directing Ch. 23) is about the
   film's *visual world* — which correctly grounds the poster's **content and mood** —
   while poster *composition* is graphic design. Decision: **ground the seat on
   production design and render the poster as "the Production Designer's look, as one
   evocative frame,"** rather than chasing a separate poster-design source (avoids a 10th
   book).

5. **Watch the story-space crowding.** Taxonomy + Directing + now Glebas all touch story.
   That is *depth, not redundancy* **only** if extraction is targeted at Glebas'
   distinctive visual-storytelling material and skips what merely restates Rabiger. Do
   the signal-to-noise **TOC skim first** (authoritative ≠ usable), and **log the overlaps
   up front** so the abridgement stays surgical.

6. **Naming guard.** Glebas' folder (`directing the story/`) is **not** the existing
   Rabiger folder (`directing/`). Keep them distinct in the catalog and INDEX to avoid
   confusion — one is a story-artist's visual-storytelling manual, the other the
   Director-centric film-directing spine.

## Process (when the deliverables land)

  subagents (full reads → write `reference/` directly + a source `INDEX.md`), which is
