# Chapter 8 — Memory Colors: Skin Tone, Skies, and Foliage

> Abridged from Alexis Van Hurkman, *Color Correction Handbook*, Ch. 8.
> **Scope:** The audience's remembered "correct" colours for the big three memory colours — **skin tone**, **skies**, and **foliage/greens** — and how to judge and target them. The colorist's *target palette*: viewer preference over photometric accuracy, always bound to the scene's illuminant.

## Memory colours & viewer preference

- **Memory colours** = the colours we recall in association with familiar objects. Across decades of research (Bartleson et al.) a handful of subjects show tight, statistically shared preferences — most reliably **skin tone, green grass, blue sky**, the three regions experts most consistently isolate for **secondary correction**.
- **Preference ≠ reality.** Viewers generally prefer *more saturated* memory colours than the measured original: grass more green/blue-green, sky more cyan and pure, skin a touch more **yellow/golden**. Skin is the closest memory colour to its real measurement, yet still remembered warmer.
- **Colour preference (hue-only):** green–cyan–blues are broadly liked; greenish-yellows and magenta-purples disliked ("if it's purple, someone's gonna die"). But **lightness and saturation matter more than hue** — the experienced colorist tunes *saturation and contrast* per hue rather than swapping colours. Beware over-desaturating shadows (people like a little colour even in low/low regions).
- **Cultural variation** exists but is minor and easily overridden by individual client preference; images **with faces** show the tightest cross-cultural agreement. Know your postproduction market's trend (e.g. pale vs. tanned) rather than assuming.
- Use these preferences as **general guidelines, not hard rules** — an *awareness* of expectation lets you play *into* it (a romantic beat) or *gently against* it (unease) deliberately.

## The naturalness constraint & image quality

- **Don't forget the illuminant.** Memory colours must stay **compatible with the scene's dominant light source**; skin/sky/foliage graded to "ideal" but *incompatible with the illuminant* read as a bad composite. The most critical object's colour becomes the audience's benchmark for whether the whole scene looks natural.
- Viewers tolerate **warm/cool (daylight) illuminant shifts** far more than **red/green** shifts — a lever for ease vs. unease within a naturalistic palette.
- **Quality ≈ Naturalness + Colorfulness + Discriminability** (Yendrikhovskij): a balance of natural rendition, an attractive amount of colour, and legibility. Viewers slightly favour *more colourful* even when slightly less natural.
- **Colorfulness** is *perceived*, not just saturation: lighter, larger, and higher-colour-contrast subjects read as more colourful. There's a sharp **threshold** past which more colour lowers perceived quality — roughly constant across subjects.
- **Discriminability** = how easily the eye reads the important subjects; driven by luma contrast (sharpness/edges, Ch. 3), colour contrast (Ch. 4), and vignettes to defeat equiluminance (Ch. 6). Also don't over-flatten a subject's natural colour variation — a banana isn't one colour, and neither is a face.

## Skin tone — what it is

- **Half the job is skin.** We're acutely sensitive to healthy complexion; slight casts read as sick (yellow/green), sunburnt/drunk (red). Everyone's skin falls in a **narrow wedge on the vectorscope** near the **flesh-tone line / I-bar** (the in-phase axis, a documented rule-of-thumb signpost for the *centre* of ideal skin hue — skin sits *near* it, not always *on* it).
- **What makes skin colour:** melanin (**eumelanin** brown→black, the main regional differentiator; **pheomelanin** red→yellow), **hemoglobin** in the dermis (the shared red that makes all complexions similar in hue), β-carotene (yellow/orange), and subsurface **scattering** off collagen/fat (skin's luminous, translucent quality). Heavier pigmentation absorbs more light → *slightly lower saturation*, not a big hue change.
- **Makeup** is physical colour-correction (foundation ≈ midtone rebalance; blush = targeted warmth — don't eyedropper it as representative skin). Matte the whole face (minus eyes) when adjusting a full makeup scheme.

## Skin tone — categories, hue, saturation, lightness

- **Complexion categories** (by vectorscope angle around the I-bar, golden→ruddy; loosely tracking the Fitzpatrick scale): **golden/Asian** (left of I-bar), **olive** (just left), **medium/dark** (on the I-bar), **pale/fair** (right, often desaturated, occasionally slight blue cast), **ruddy/mahogany** (right, most saturated). Bad **spray-tans** = excess saturation → desaturate. DSC Labs charts distil "African/Asian/Caucasian/Indian" averages into four reference patches.
- **Hue:** RGB parade shows **red > green > blue** for all skin; hue varies ~5° even within one face. Correct casts on **Whites**, fine-tune complexion on **Midtones** (skin usually lives in the midtones).
- **Saturation:** ordinarily **~20–50 % amplitude** on the vectorscope. Fashionably pale ≈ 20–30 %; average ≈ 25–40 % (~35 %); ruddy/tanned ≈ 35–50 % (40 % a sane ceiling). Very dark complexions can land as *low* as pale ones (melanin absorbs light).
- **Lightness (exposure):** keep the most important subject **clearly visible** — skin tones are your "vocals" in the mix; they need to be *distinct*, not brightest. Rough starting ranges: highlights 60–90 %, midtones 40–70 % (dark→light), shadows 10–50 % (avoid ugly hard clipping). Darker complexions read ~10–20 % darker in the same light — normal, no fix needed. Adams' zone system: Caucasian skin spans roughly Zones 4–8.
- **The colour of emotion:** genuine upset flushes the face redder — usually *leave the performance in*, matching flush shot-to-shot; ease off only for documentary continuity or an already-beet-red subject. Our M/L cones are tuned to hemoglobin oxygenation, so we read blush (red), fear (yellow/pale), choking (purple) as mood/health cues — small hue/sat skin shifts are legible audience signals.

## Skin tone — adjustment techniques (compressed)

- **Primary first:** play highlights against midtones — set the overall look with **Gain**, then tame complexion with **Gamma** (and a small opposite **Lift** move to protect blacks). Exhaust the three balance controls before reaching for a secondary — it's faster.
- **Contrast:** a gentle highlight boost (windowed) smooths freckles/blemishes; luma-only contrast "ruggedizes" skin (moles/wrinkles). When high contrast **crushes hair**, HSL-key the hair (darkest, least-saturated region), invert, and add back *some* contrast to it separately.
- **Hue curves:** *Hue vs. Hue* / *Hue vs. Sat* on the sliver of skin hue — nudge toward red for robustness, desaturate/blend a blotchy complexion (a little goes a long way; a *perfect* match looks unnaturally flat).
- **HSL Qualification (secondary):** for extreme grades or two mismatched actors; reds/oranges are ubiquitous, so combine the key with a **limiting shape / Power Window** (+ tracking) and stay near the scene illuminant. **Protect skin from stylized environments** by splitting into two secondaries off one inverted mask (grade background cool, then re-integrate the subject so she catches some of the new light). "Copy skin forward" from an early node when a deep grade stack has wrecked the complexion.
- Lore worth carrying: **keep colour in skin shadows** (mix a complementary, don't just add black/desaturate — over-desaturated shadows go grey/ashen); the **"traffic-light" face** (yellowish forehead, reddish cheeks/nose, faint greenish chin) adds dimension; render **detail selectively** to steer the eye; a few **exceptional (specular) highlights** on nose/lip/eye give gloss — use sparingly. On **orange-and-teal**: colour *separation* under aggressive grading isn't a crime; the failures are **oversaturated** or **overprotected** (illuminant-ignoring) skin.

## Skies

- **Rayleigh scattering** makes the clear sky a **gradient**: darkest/most-saturated at the zenith, lightening and desaturating toward the horizon. So across the frame **hue stays roughly constant, saturation peaks at top, brightness peaks at the horizon** (the inverse of saturation).
- **Hue** ≈ HSB 200 (cyan) → 220 (blue); the horizon may tint from surface reflection (dark over water, greenish over vegetation, brownish-yellow over desert). **Saturation** ~50–60 % at the zenith → ~0 at a level horizon. **Brightness** never at 100 % for a rich blue (white point is reserved for desaturated highlights). Gradient **angle** follows the sun/camera relationship; a **shot-reverse-shot** across the sun can leave the two skies visibly different — match if it distracts.
- **Other sky states:** **sunsets** = long red wavelengths (clear air → yellow; particulate/pollution/salt → orange/red/peach; clouds → striated). **Clouds** are desaturated white/grey (reflection, not scatter), cooler on overcast days (~8000 K vs ~6500 K north-sky). **Haze** = **Mie scattering** (all wavelengths, white glare, low contrast) — minimise by expanding contrast (drop shadows/midtones, hold highlights). Shooting aids: **polarizers** (darken/saturate + cut haze/reflections — capture-only) and **graduated ND / colour filters** (simulate in post with vignettes).
- **Adjustment:** fastest is **Hue vs. Hue / Hue vs. Sat** curves (blue is well-separated) — nudge cyan→blue and lift saturation, but too far → neon/purple. Or **HSL-qualify** the sky (luma sits in the midtones, so a narrow luma band keys it) when curves catch other blues or there's motion. **Vignettes** add or steepen a gradient into a flat/white sky (post-filter that follows the natural angle). Always: a little goes a long way; watch for illegal/neon blue.

## Foliage

- Green comes from **chlorophyll** (absorbs violet-blue and orange-red, reflects green); secondary pigments **carotene** (yellow, more stable — shows in sickly leaves) and **anthocyanins** (red/purple, soil-pH dependent). Younger leaves are briefly **yellow-green**; mature/older leaves **blue-green**.
- **Preference:** we *remember* foliage yellow-green but *prefer* blue-green. Plant-breeder ideals agree — **darker/richer green = healthier and more convincing**; yellow adult leaves read as sick; minimise variegation. Lighter greens **go neon** fast unless desaturated (which then loses colour contrast). Golden-hour light legitimately yellows translucent leaf highlights — make it clear the illuminant is doing it (not a viral infection).
- **Adjustment:** foliage's fine, wind-blown detail makes HSL keys chatter, so **hue curves** are usually best (green is well-separated) — a *few degrees* shift toward blue-green + a saturation trim; more than that looks bizarre. Use **HSL + a limiting shape** only when correcting a *subset* of greens (e.g. ivy but not grass). **Autumn:** enhance vivid fall colour with saturation + Hue-vs-Sat (watch red for broadcast-legal); *subduing* fall colour toward green is doable-but-hard (key reds/yellows/purples → rebalance to match the scene's real greens on the vectorscope); *adding* colour that was never there is a VFX/compositing job.

## Studio application

- **Memory colours are the studio's target palette** — the colour analogue of the DP's compositional defaults. Just as each shoot role's `heuristic(brief)` picks sane defaults for its owned fields ([camera.py](../../../sequitur/crew/camera.py), [lighting.py](../../../sequitur/crew/lighting.py)), a future **Colorist**'s deterministic heuristic (the "A" [HeuristicJudgment](../../../sequitur/crew/judgment.py)) would target skin/sky/foliage: **skin near the flesh-tone/I-bar wedge at ~20–50 % saturation**, **blue sky as a top-dark gradient**, **foliage rich/deep-green**. The chapter's hue/saturation/lightness ranges are literally the numbers such a heuristic (or a typed target-palette table it consults) would encode.
- **The naturalness constraint is a `Contribution` invariant.** A grade must stay compatible with the scene illuminant — so a Colorist decision reads the same `Brief.hints`/`mood` the shoot crew read ([role.py](../../../sequitur/crew/role.py)), and its memory-colour targets are always *relative to* the Gaffer's chosen colour temperature. This is the studio's ethos in colour form: **viewer preference over photometric accuracy** (grade for how the scene *felt*, more saturated than reality), matching the "grammar, not literal capture" stance everywhere else.
- **A future QC seam.** "Skin/sky legality" (broadcast-safe reds, no neon blue/green) is a **validate() analogue** — the colour counterpart of `Sequence.validate()` in [edit.py](../../../sequitur/edit.py) and the sound-layer QC — and connects directly to [Ch. 10 — Quality Control and Broadcast Safe](ch10-quality-control-and-broadcast-safe.md). Memory-colour targets are the *aim*; legality is the *guardrail*.
- **Overlap — the vectorscope flesh-tone line (Ch. 4).** The I-bar / flesh-tone line is introduced as a primary-colour tool in [Ch. 4 — Primary Color Adjustments](ch04-primary-color-adjustments.md); here it becomes the skin **target**. That line is the single most reused colour landmark in the book and the anchor of any skin heuristic.
- **Overlap — the Production Designer palette/lookbook seat.** Memory-colour targets are exactly the kind of reference palette the **Production Designer** cell (an *open* art/colour seat in [architecture.md](../../../context/architecture.md), currently only partial via `ImageStudio`) would own — a sensor/reference-lookup renderer, distinct from the Colorist's transform-flavor grade. The two share the palette but differ in flavor (decide-the-palette vs. apply-the-grade).
- **Overlap — the Gaffer's `eye_light` / skin rendering.** Skin is lit *from within*, so its on-screen colour is inseparable from the capture lighting — the same **`ColorTemperature` two-seat overlap** flagged for [Ch. 7](ch07-animating-grades.md): the Gaffer sets it at capture ([lighting.py](../../../sequitur/crew/lighting.py)), the Colorist re-targets it at grade. Memory colours are where that hand-off has to be reconciled.
