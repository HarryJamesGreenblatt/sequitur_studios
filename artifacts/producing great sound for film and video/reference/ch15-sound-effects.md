# Chapter 15 — Sound Effects

> Abridged from Jay Rose, *Producing Great Sound for Film and Video* (4th ed.),
> Ch. 15.
> **Scope:** effect categories, sourcing, sync placement, looping, and layering.
> The `SoundDesigner`'s toolkit — and a second concrete home for the
> diegetic/non-diegetic split.

## Core idea

They're called **sound effects, not sound truths** — don't follow real-world
rules, follow the story. Effects are the **cheapest professional-flavor boost**,
but **add them only after dialog and music are locked**, and **silence can be
golden** — don't force effects a scene doesn't need.

## Two categories

| Category | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Reality effects** | invisible; build belief the world was captured live (footsteps, ambience, off-screen dog) |
| **Punctuation effects** | deliberately call attention (rim shot, whoosh, logo) — accepted as entertainment, not realism |

Many sounds are **dual-role** by placement (chimes = doorbell *or* a cartoon
"idea"). The real world is **never silent** — fill it with an appropriate
background.

## Choosing and placing

- **Hear it in your head first**, then **break it into components** ("cellblock
  door" = keys + sliding gate, slowed + echo) and **ignore the file name** (audition
  by sound).
- **Acoustics must match:** an exterior slam + echo can imitate an interior; the
  reverse is nearly impossible. Roll off high-mids to add distance.
- **Sync to the visible frame** (barrel flash, fist contact) — snap the sound's
  front or middle to that frame; **don't compensate for sound travel** (viewers
  expect instant sync). In fast cuts with no room, placing **1–2 frames ahead** of
  the visual smooths the rhythm. Cue an explosion to its **crunch**, not the
  rumble start.
- **Don't let two equal hard sounds compete** (gun + crash = mush); layer **highs +
  lows** and keep the midrange clear for dialog.

## Looping and layering

- **Overlap-crossfade** on alternate tracks hides a splice; a **C-loop** (reversed
  second copy) seamlessly loops a *timbre-changing* background.
- **Make effects bigger by layering, not bass EQ** (which muddies): double-record
  and **pitch a copy down to ~80%** for weight; spread copies L/R for width; always
  **check the result in mono**.

## Studio application

Provisional leads for the studio's sound layer:

- **Reality vs. punctuation *is* the diegetic/non-diegetic split, in effect form.**
  Reality effects = **diegetic** (belong to the story world, `SoundMixer`/
  `SoundDesigner` keep them coherent with the shot's space); punctuation effects =
  **non-diegetic** commentary (`SoundDesigner` post layer). This gives the split a
  second concrete home beyond dialog (ch. 4) — the taxonomy the sound department
  is organized by.
- **"Add effects only after dialog + music lock; silence is valid"** reinforces the
  ch. 10 priority order and makes **silence a first-class, choosable state** in the
  layer, not an absence.
- **Sync placement rules are `SoundDesigner` primitives:** snap to the action
  frame, allow 1–2 frames early only in fast cuts, cue to the crunch — encodable
  heuristics the `SoundAnalyst`→Editor path can apply. (Note the contrast with
  ch. 13's *dialog* rule that sound must never be early — effects get a small
  exception for editing rhythm.)
- **Layer/loop/C-loop and "match acoustics to the shot"** are the design ops that,
  like ch. 6's perspective model, tie an effect's **space** to the framing.
