# Chapter 4 — Genre Case Studies

> Abridged from Eric R. Williams, *The Screenwriter's Taxonomy*, Ch. 4.
> **Scope:** a demonstration that **an idea does not dictate genre** — one logline,
> parsed three ways with the first three layers, becomes three very different films.

## Core idea

Return to Ch. 1's pitch: *"A man, falsely imprisoned, falls victim to the very worst
in human behavior before finding his way to freedom and putting his life back
together."* Williams shows that **the same through-line** is the skeleton of three
acclaimed, utterly different films — the difference lives entirely in the
**Supergenre / Macro / Micro** each writer chose.

| Film | Supergenre | Macros → Micros | What the choice buys |
|------|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| *The Shawshank Redemption* | **Crime** | Bromance→Traditional · Survival→Captivity · Escape→Tale | reads as friendship/survival; the *escape* macro is hidden until the Act-III double-cross |
| *Twelve Monkeys* | **Science Fiction** | Apocalyptic→Preapocalypse · Time Travel→Exists · Identity→Humanity | the three macros marry into one mind-bending, self-sacrificing ending |
| *12 Years a Slave* | **Slice-of-Life** | Biographical→Temporal · Survival→Captivity · Psychological→Worst Case | honesty over Hollywood catharsis; "the preciousness of life" |

## The craft lesson

- **The super choice cascades.** Ridley's decision to make *12 Years a Slave* a
  Slice-of-Life (not the "obvious" escape-thriller his first instinct wanted) forced a
  **primary-limited POV** and demanded *realism* — he deliberately refused the
  crowd-pleasing "I'm not leaving without her" beat because it "wasn't Solomon's
  circumstance." Genre discipline *is* story discipline.
- **Macros can disguise each other.** Darabont spends ~100 pages on Bromance +
  Survival precisely so the Escape macro lands as a reveal. The layering is a
  *withholding* device.
- **Genre is a shaping tool, not a straitjacket.** The taxonomy didn't generate these
  stories; it gave each writer the form to give one idea "shape and substance."

## Studio application

- **This chapter is the argument for putting the Screenwriter *upstream*.** The same
  logline → three genre configurations → three films is the plan-phase proof that the
  **genre selection is the highest-leverage decision** in the pipeline: it propagates
  into POV (→ camera), pacing (→ editor), realism/atmosphere (→ production design &
  sound). A `Screenwriter` `Contribution` therefore belongs *before* the shoot crew in
  the `Engine`'s phase order, seeding the `Brief` the `Director` reconciles
  ([storyline 0014](../../../context/storyline/0014-the-crew-behaviour.md)).
- **"Super choice cascades to POV" is a concrete cross-layer rule.** Ridley:
  Slice-of-Life → primary-limited. That is a *derivation* a heuristic can encode —
  pick a supergenre, and default a compatible [Point of View](ch07-point-of-view.md)
  and [Pathway](ch06-pathway.md) unless overridden. The layers aren't independent; the
  case studies expose their dependencies.
- **"Macro as withholding device" ties story to the edit.** Darabont hiding the Escape
  macro is a *reveal* — the same withhold-for-suspense logic the
  [Grammar of the Edit](../../grammar%20of%20the%20edit/reference/ch05-when-to-cut.md)
  cut-decision model already encodes. Genre layering and cut motivation are two ends of
  one narrative-tension knob.
